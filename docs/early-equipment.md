# Equipment and workshop delivery before zone-key chains

The first ten-seed report found that a nearby equipment location could still be
blocked by a key from late in the seed. Spatial placement rules alone did not
meet the intended opening balance. This follow-up uses actual initial AP state.

For fresh content_progression=1/playability_logic=1 activity pools, remaining local
equipment and workshop permissions are placed on reliable, low-grind checks in the selected
starting region or global activities accessible there. Nearby maps within two
transitions are also eligible when already reachable without new zone keys; the
starting region takes priority. This preserves small custom pools with unlocked routes. Starting-map arrival is
reserved for ordinary zone-key fill when keys are enabled. The planner prioritises
PDA, firearms, detector and melee, preferring checks already reachable before any
new permission. Further permissions can use the capabilities gained from those
receipts. It plans the whole local allocation before changing locations or pool.
Random choice among valid checks keeps these opening placements seed-dependent.

Workshop Access: Repair, Crafting and Upgrades use the same early allocation as
gear. They cannot sit behind late rank, deep artefacts, upgrades or 15,000-RU
contracts. PDA/firearms/detector/melee retain their priority; workshops join the
remaining equipment permissions. Higher tool tiers and recipe-group access keep
their independent requirements and placement rules. Early workshop permission
does not waive native tools, parts, recipe knowledge or payment requirements.

No zone key, faction goodwill, melter or tool-tier stash is assumed by this plan.
Excluded locations and item rules remain enforced. Explicit non-local permissions
use upstream early_items requests instead of being forced into the local slot;
other players' timing remains outside a local guarantee. Gifts and prior plando
placements are respected. Sparse map/settlement-only pools retain the existing
exploration fallback. Incompatible activity pools fail clearly, offering more
low-grind checks, fewer restrictions or legacy playability assumptions.

Thirty focused seed/start combinations test Cordon, Clear Sky and Rostok and show
all nine local equipment and three workshop permissions reachable within three
capability waves without collecting zone keys. The earlier ten actual Cordon archives,
which predate early workshop placement, put PDA/firearms/detector
in the first wave and all equipment in the second. Waves are not measured hours;
travel, money earning, encounters and multiworld waiting still require playtests.
Other reward categories retain ordinary Archipelago fill, including keys.
Current sessions and native inventory are not modified by generation rules.
